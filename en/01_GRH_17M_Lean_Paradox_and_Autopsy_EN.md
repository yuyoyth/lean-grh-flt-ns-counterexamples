## Chapter 3　Primary Graph-Theoretic Theorem 1.4: The Semantic Defect Conservation and Extraction Trilemma Theorem from Original Proof Graph $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orig}}$ to Pruned Graph $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{ext}}$ 

In discussions surrounding multi-million-line formalizations, one frequently encounters a seductive defensive claim: "Even if the original seventeen-million-line codebase contains redundant modules, convoluted proof terms, or local flaws, so long as one prunes the import tree, deletes unreferenced modules, and shortens selected proof bodies to obtain a smaller extracted repository that passes the typechecker, any defects in the original repository become irrelevant."

To settle once and for all what pruning or extracting a directed proof dependency graph can and cannot achieve mathematically, we establish **Theorem 1.4 (The Semantic Defect Conservation and Extraction Trilemma Theorem)** on finite directed acyclic declaration dependency graphs (where each mutual inductive block is contracted into a single supernode).

### 3.1　Rigorous Definition of the Directed Proof Dependency Graph, Upstream Dependency Cone, and Graph Extraction Operator

Let the declaration dependency graph in a static type-theoretic environment be $\mathcal{G} = (V, E, \tau, b, \Gamma)$ , where the vertex set $V$ consists of all formal declarations in the environment (theorems, lemmas, definitions, inductive types, constructors, recursors, and axioms), and a directed edge $(u \to v) \in E$ records that declaration $v$ directly references declaration $u$ in its type expression $\tau(v)$ , its definition or proof body $b(v)$ , or its inductive structural rules. For any target theorem $v_{\mathrm{goal}} \in V$ , its **upstream dependency cone** is the set of all vertices from which there exists a directed path in $\mathcal{G}$ reaching $v_{\mathrm{goal}}$ :

$$
\operatorname{Cone}_{\mathcal{G}}(v_{\mathrm{goal}}) := \bigl\{u \in V : u \leadsto_{\mathcal{G}} v_{\mathrm{goal}}\bigr\} \qquad \bigl(v_{\mathrm{goal}} \in \operatorname{Cone}_{\mathcal{G}}(v_{\mathrm{goal}})\bigr). \tag{3.1}
$$

Let $E_{\mathrm{err}} \subseteq V$ denote a subset of problematic or excised declarations in the original graph $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orig}}$ . A **graph extraction or pruning operator**

$$
\mathcal{E} : \bigl(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orig}},\,v_{\mathrm{goal}}\bigr) \longmapsto \bigl(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{ext}},\,v_{\mathrm{goal}}^{\mathrm{ext}},\,\mu,\,\mathcal{B}\bigr) \tag{3.2}

### 3.2　Theorem 1.4 (Semantic Defect Conservation and the Extraction Trilemma Under a Frozen Weak Quotient Signature)

Suppose the original graph $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orig}}$ claims to prove the weak-quotient factorized target $\forall x \in X,\ C(q(x)) \to Z(x)$ , and an extracted graph $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{ext}}$ removes a subset $E_{\mathrm{err}}$ from $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orig}}$ while claiming to prove the same target without introducing new arithmetic separation premises capable of distinguishing $x_0$ from $x_1$ .
